# Step 1: The request

> **Scenario note.** The data in this project is real (Hillstrom, 2008). The company's people, messages and business figures in these documents are invented to show how the experiment would be designed in practice. People are referred to by role only.

---

**From:** Head of E-mail Marketing
**To:** Data Science
**Subject:** Test for the two new spring campaigns?

Hi,

We have two new campaigns ready: one featuring our men's range and one featuring the women's range. The plan is to send them to the whole active file (everyone who bought in the last 12 months, about 640,000 customers) in April and then make two campaigns a month our standard rhythm.

The CFO has asked us to prove that email actually drives sales before he signs off next year's email budget (see his note below). Our weekly report credits email with about $1.2M of revenue last quarter, but he doesn't accept that number.

Could you set up a test so we can answer him? We'd like to know:

1. Do the emails bring in sales we wouldn't otherwise get?
2. Which of the two campaigns works better?
3. Ideally, who we should send each one to.

We think these campaigns are strong. Based on past campaigns, our gut says they lift purchases by around 30%.

Thanks!

---

**Forwarded note from the CFO:**

> The e-mail report counts every purchase made by anyone who received an email. But the people on that list are our most recent customers. Many of them would have bought anyway. I don't want to know how much revenue *followed* an email; I want to know how much was *caused* by it.
>
> Creative production costs us about $8,000 per campaign. At two a month, that's close to $200,000 a year before sending costs. Show me the emails pay for themselves and I'll approve the budget.
